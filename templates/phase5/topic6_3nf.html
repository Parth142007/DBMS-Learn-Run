6. Third Normal Form (3NF) & Transitive Dependencies

Eliminate transitive dependencies across non-key attributes — master both the intuitive definition (no non-prime attribute depends transitively on a key) and the formal Codd/Zaniolo 3NF definition ($X$ is a superkey OR $A$ is a prime attribute).

6.1

Intuitive vs. Formal Definitions of 3NF

1. Intuitive Definition (Kent & Codd)
No Transitive Dependencies

A relation is in 3NF if it is in 2NF and no non-prime attribute is transitively dependent on any candidate key.

"Every non-key attribute must provide a fact about the key, the whole key (2NF), and nothing but the key (3NF)!"

2. Formal Rigorous Definition
Codd & Zaniolo 3NF Condition

A relational schema $R$ is in 3NF if, for every non-trivial functional dependency $X \to A$, at least ONE of the following is true:

  • Condition 1: $X$ is a superkey of $R$, OR
  • Condition 2: $A$ is a prime attribute of $R$ (member of some candidate key).
Crucial Distinction: Condition 2 is What Makes 3NF Special! Many beginner textbooks oversimplify 3NF by claiming that "every determinant on the left must be a candidate key". That is BCNF, not 3NF! 3NF explicitly relaxes the rule: if the determinant $X$ is NOT a superkey, the dependency $X \to A$ is still completely legal in 3NF as long as the dependent attribute $A$ is a prime attribute!
6.2

Case Study: The Student-Department Transitive Violation

Consider a student academic table that includes department details:

STUDENT(StudentID, StudentName, DepartmentID, DepartmentName)

Domain Functional Dependencies:

1. StudentID → StudentName, DepartmentID        Valid (StudentID is Superkey)
2. DepartmentID → DepartmentName                         Violates 3NF!
⇒ Transitive Dependency: StudentID → DepartmentID → DepartmentName
Testing DepartmentID → DepartmentName Against 3NF Rules:
  • Is DepartmentID a superkey of STUDENT? NO (multiple students belong to the same department).
  • Is DepartmentName a prime attribute? NO (the only candidate key is StudentID).
  • Since neither condition holds, the table strictly violates Third Normal Form!
The 3NF Decomposition Solution:

Extract the non-key determinant and its dependent into a separate relation, leaving the determinant behind as a foreign key:

Relation 1: STUDENT (3NF)
StudentID (PK) StudentName DepartmentID (FK)
101AmitD01
102NehaD01
103PoojaD02
FD: StudentID → StudentName, DepartmentID (Key determines all)
Relation 2: DEPARTMENT (3NF)
DepartmentID (PK) DepartmentName
D01Computer Science
D02Mechanical Eng
FD: DepartmentID → DepartmentName (DeptID is primary key!)

Exercise 6.2 Formal 3NF Prime Attribute Rule

When is a Non-Superkey Determinant Permitted in 3NF?

In relation $R(A, B, C)$ with candidate keys $(A, B)$ and $(A, C)$, suppose there is an FD $B \to C$. Does $B \to C$ violate 3NF?

Hint: NO, it does NOT violate 3NF! Because $C$ is a member of candidate key $(A, C)$, $C$ is a prime attribute! Under 3NF Condition 2, any dependency where the dependent is a prime attribute is valid in 3NF (though it will violate BCNF!).
