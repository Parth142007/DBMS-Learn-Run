Lesson 3

Relationships, Degree & Recursive Roles

NEPC42 Core Concept
1. Definition of Relationship and Degree

In relational data modeling, an entity rarely exists in isolation. A relationship is an association among two or more entities. The degree of a relationship is defined as the number of participating entity types in that relationship set.

Degree 1
Unary (Recursive) Relationship

Occurs when an entity type participates in a relationship with itself. Different instances of the same entity set assume distinct roles.

Real-World Example: A senior STUDENT (Role: Mentor) mentors a junior STUDENT (Role: Mentee); or an EMPLOYEE (Role: Manager) manages another EMPLOYEE (Role: Subordinate).
Degree 2
Binary Relationship

The most common relationship type in database engineering, connecting two distinct entity types.

Real-World Example: STUDENT Enrolls In COURSE; or INSTRUCTOR Heads DEPARTMENT.
Degree 3+
Ternary (N-ary) Relationship

Involves three (or N) distinct entity types simultaneously binding a complex multi-way business fact.

Real-World Example: SUPPLIER supplies a PART for a specific PROJECT.
2. Role Names in Unary (Recursive) Relationships

When an entity relates to itself, the meaning of each connection becomes ambiguous unless explicit role names are labeled on the diagram lines. Role names explain the specific function an entity instance performs in each direction:

Unary / Recursive ER Diagram: One Student serves in the Mentor role, while multiple junior students serve in the Mentee role. In MySQL 8, this maps to a Self-Referencing Foreign Key: mentor_id INT REFERENCES students(student_id).
3. Why a Ternary Relationship Cannot Always Be Replaced by Three Binary Relationships
Crucial Exam & Interview Concept (Semantic Loss):
Students often mistakenly ask: "Why do we need ternary relationships? Can't we just create three binary relationships (Supplier-Part, Part-Project, Supplier-Project)?"
Answer: NO! Decomposing a ternary relationship into three binary relationships frequently causes severe loss of semantic meaning (false inference / spurious associations).
Proof by Counterexample: The Supplier-Part-Project Problem
Three Separate Binary Relationships:

Suppose we only record three independent binary facts:

  • Supplier S1 supplies Part P1.
  • Part P1 is used in Project J1.
  • Supplier S1 works on Project J1.
The Fatal Flaw: These three binary facts DO NOT prove that Supplier S1 supplied Part P1 FOR Project J1! Supplier S1 might only supply Part P2 to Project J1, while Supplier S2 supplied Part P1.
Single Ternary Relationship:

A single 3-way atomic association SUPPLIES(SupplierID, PartID, ProjectID, Quantity):

  • The tuple (S1, P1, J1, 500) explicitly records that Supplier S1 delivered 500 units of Part P1 specifically for Project J1.
Semantic Guarantee: Simultaneous three-way binding preserves true business reality without spurious connections.
