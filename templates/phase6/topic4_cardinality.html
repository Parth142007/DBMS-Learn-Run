Lesson 4

Cardinality Constraints (1:1, 1:N, N:1, M:N)

NEPC42 Core Concept
1. Mapping Cardinalities (Maximum Cardinality)

Mapping Cardinality (or cardinality ratio) expresses the maximum number of entities of one entity set to which another entity can be associated via a relationship set. It is a critical constraint that dictates how tables and foreign keys are created in SQL.

Critical Distinction: Maximum Cardinality vs. Minimum Participation:
Maximum Cardinality (1 or Many) defines the upper bound of connections an entity can have.
Minimum Participation (0 or 1) defines whether participation is mandatory (Total) or optional (Partial).
Never confuse the two! A 1:N relationship can have optional participation on either or both sides.
1. One-to-One (1:1) Ratio 1:1

An entity in entity set $A$ is associated with at most one entity in $B$, and an entity in $B$ is associated with at most one entity in $A$.

College Example: INSTRUCTOR Heads DEPARTMENT.
Each Department has exactly one Head (Instructor), and an Instructor can head at most one Department.
Department (A)HeadsInstructor (B)
D10 (Computer)↔I101 (Dr. Sharma)
D20 (Electronics)↔I102 (Dr. Verma)
Schema Impact: Put Foreign Key in either table (typically on the table with total participation) with a UNIQUE constraint on the FK column.
2. One-to-Many (1:N) Ratio 1:N

An entity in $A$ can be associated with any number ($\ge 0$) of entities in $B$, but an entity in $B$ is associated with at most one entity in $A$.

College Example: DEPARTMENT Employs INSTRUCTOR.
One department employs many instructors, but each instructor is appointed to only one department.
Department (1)EmploysInstructors (N)
D10 (Computer)→I101 (Dr. Sharma)
→I103 (Prof. Gupta)
D20 (Electronics)→I102 (Dr. Verma)
Schema Impact: Always place the Foreign Key on the Many ($N$) side: INSTRUCTOR(..., department_id FK)!
3. Many-to-One (N:1) Ratio N:1

The mirror of 1:N: Multiple entities in $A$ associate with at most one entity in $B$.

College Example: STUDENT Belongs To DEPARTMENT.
Many students belong to one department; each student has exactly one home department.
Students (N)Belongs ToDepartment (1)
101 (Amit)→D10 (Computer)
103 (Ravi)→
102 (Neha)→D20 (Electronics)
Schema Impact: Foreign Key placed on STUDENT(..., department_id FK).
4. Many-to-Many (M:N) Ratio M:N

An entity in $A$ is associated with any number of entities in $B$, and an entity in $B$ is associated with any number of entities in $A$.

College Example: STUDENT Enrolls In COURSE.
A student registers for multiple courses, and each course enrolls multiple students.
Student (M)Enrolls InCourse (N)
101 (Amit)↔C101 (DBMS)
101 (Amit)↔C102 (Python)
102 (Neha)↔C101 (DBMS)
Schema Impact: CANNOT place a foreign key directly in either table! Requires a Separate Junction / Associative Table ENROLLMENT(student_id PK/FK, course_id PK/FK)!
2. Graphical Notation: Chen vs. Crow's Foot
Chen Style (NEPC42)

Numbers and letters (1, N, M) are written explicitly on the line segments connecting the entity rectangle to the relationship diamond.

[STUDENT] --- (1) --- <Belongs_To> --- (N) --- [DEPARTMENT]
[STUDENT] --- (M) --- <Enrolls_In> --- (N) --- [COURSE]
Crow's Foot Style (Industry Standard)

Uses three prongs at the end of the connecting line to denote "Many", a straight tick mark for "One", and a circle for "Zero / Optional".

DEPARTMENT ||----------|< INSTRUCTOR (1:N)
STUDENT >|----------|< COURSE (M:N)
